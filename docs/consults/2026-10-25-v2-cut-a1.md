# V2 cut A1: memory addresses formed in S (2026-10-25)

The first RTL step of the 104 MHz plan
([2026-10-25-v2-104mhz-plan.md](2026-10-25-v2-104mhz-plan.md), §1 A1).

**Commits:**
- test `64ae85c` (`sim/cpu/tests/arm9_sagu_rtl.S`);
- RTL `2554624` (`arm9v2_core/decode/front_decode/execute.sv`, the
  regenerated netlist, the `ldr_base` window model).

Nothing was pushed, and nothing went to the board.

## What changed

The plan's cut: an address adder in S, registered into A, so that A
drives the D-cache and TLB lookup from a register.
- **The parameter:** `S_AGU`, default 1 in `arm9v2_core`, 0 in the
  modules. ZAP is untouched.
- **The adder (`arm9v2_execute.sv`):** S sums the base and the immediate
  or register offset from S's selected (registered-producer or RF)
  operands. It covers LDR/STR, halfword and signed forms, the SWP base,
  and a block transfer's base plus offset. Later block transfers take the
  first transfer's base from A's record or `block_base`.
- **The registers:** the address and the writeback sum are registered
  into A as `a.agu` and `a.agu_sum`. `o_address` is `a.agu` for **every
  kind**, because the core's RUN-state lookup takes A's address for any
  instruction. The ALU-to-RAM chain is therefore gone even for
  speculative lookups.
- **Decode (`arm9v2_decode.sv`):** the base, a register offset, the SWP
  base and a block's first base become early (S-needed) sources. The
  block's early flag clears with `used[0]` after the first transfer.
- **Scaled register offsets:** one prepared S clock, as for a
  register-specified shift. The rotated offset is registered in
  `held_offset`, then summed. `shift_amount` takes the held amount only
  for `i_reg_shift`, so the logic without S_AGU is unchanged.
- **Writeback chains** (`str r0,[r8],#4` back to back) keep their rate.
  A memory base whose writer is the scalar MEM in A takes that record's
  registered sum, provided A's condition passes, it hasn't faulted and
  it isn't an `Rd == Rn` load. D2 exempts S- or A-lane-1 MEM writers from
  the early hazard for memory bases (`i_early_agu`). `a.left` captures
  the forwarded base, so the writeback and the abort restore stay right.
  A block writeback feeding the next base still costs +1.
- **The Thumb BL suffix's target** used to travel in the record address
  and now comes from a separate ALU output (`o_alu_address`,
  `arm9v2_core.sv` `input_record.address`).

## Cycles

**Windows** (`sim/cpu/arm9v2/*-cycles.txt`, exact, both predictor
modes):
- The scalar 3/4/5 windows are unchanged: `ldr_independent` 3, `ldr_alu`
  4, `ldr_shift_value` 5.
- `stores_index`, `loads_index` and `mixed` (the writeback chains) stay

## Verification

- **`arm9_sagu_rtl`** passes on ZAP and V2 at 0:0, 0:3 and 2:9, and its
  traces match QEMU's (13,771 instructions).
  - It runs uncached, then cached cold and warm.
  - It covers every base and offset source above, plus a pending-load
    lock on the base (an uncached producer, a cold miss, a writeback
    chain whose producer misses), an MSR flush, and a chained abort (FAR
    and the restored base).
- **The test catches A1 bugs.** Two mutants fail it on V2:
  - forwarding a condition-failed writeback fails check 26;
  - taking a later block transfer's base from the S network fails check
    31.
- **QEMU:** 74/74 in all five configurations.
- **Lints:** the four configurations (`make lint`, `-Wall` on `emu` with
  `NGAGE_DEBUG`, with `NGAGE_CORE_ARM9`, and with both) are clean.

- **The component benches** (`make arm9v2-frontend/-decode/-execute`)
  already failed to build at HEAD: frontend on `o_dbg_fetch`, decode and

  were not used as gates.

## Assessment

A1 does what the plan asked of it:
- **The address families are gone:** about +2.2 and +2.4 ns on the
  address → metadata and address → TLB paths. `metadata_descriptor` and
  `short_collision` are positive.
- **The common-case windows are kept:** 3/4/5 holds.
- **The cost is as the step-0 model said:** about +0.02-0.04 % on the
  firmware boot, and +5.8 % on the pointer-chasing `cache` bench.

The worst slack moves 1.15 ns (−3.843 → −2.691) in the trial. The device
stays at −3.70, against the −0.6 ns target, because the next families
now lead:
- **B, the late bypass:** including fix 1/2's trust qualifier from
  `o_live_lookup`, which leads the whole device at −3.70.
- **C, the short response:** −2.16.
- **D, completion via `wb_flush`:** `execute`, `rf` and `locks`, −1.9 to
  −2.5.
- **E, the front end:** `decode_d1` −2.42, `values` in the device.

The plan's next steps (B1, C1, D1 and E) are all needed. The trust
qualifier, which is new since S12, should be added to B1's scope: a
registered `i_load_trust` would take `o_live_lookup` off the late
select.
