**The biggest demonstrated CPU-speed lever is eliminating TLB conflicts.** Start there, then reduce I-cache refills. Neither alone establishes a route to phone-like boot time: the 48-second idle interval is a separate problem, and several measurements need reconciliation.
All gains below are conditional estimates, not measured improvements. Nothing was modified or executed beyond read-only inspection.
First, correct the accounting:
- The idle loop executes \(48×21.1M=1.013G\) instructions—roughly half the reported boot total. Matching QEMU’s 2G count therefore does **not** establish equal useful work. QEMU’s configured instruction-based time is also not a phone performance measurement ([CPU.md:495](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/docs/CPU.md:495)).
- \(20597/999=20.62\) CPI locally, versus approximately 13 CPI from \(104/8\) for phase (c). Sampling variation can explain this; extrapolating the local instruction mix across the phase cannot be assumed.
- One two-read walk every 78 clocks implies \(2×20597/78=528\) reads. Together with 512 flash beats, that already exceeds 999, before writes. These cannot describe the same interval. The rings record independently, with different depths and timestamp widths ([dbg_trace.sv:97](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/mister/dbg_trace.sv:97), [dbg_trace.sv:167](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/mister/dbg_trace.sv:167)). Also, 134/800 is 16.8%, and 512/999 is 51.3%, not the progress log’s 39%.
- A phase extending to 203 seconds and a 180-second boot require separate run/time-origin labels.
- Bus-pending clocks are **not necessarily CPU-stalled clocks**; loads can overlap execution. The counter excludes acknowledgement clocks ([NGage.sv:1040](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/NGage.sv:1040)). Retirement gaps identify symptoms, not which preceding instruction caused them.
My ranked implementation plan follows. Resource figures are engineering budgets, not synthesis results. None of these changes should materially shorten phase (b) if its endpoint is a wall-clock timeout.
1. **Remove small-page TLB thrashing without creating a large combinational CAM.**
   Both addresses index slot zero with VA[15:12]; section-TLB enlargement cannot help small pages. A cheap first experiment is **64 small-page entries per side**: VA[17:12] separates these addresses; 32 entries still collide. For broader conflicts, use a small victim TLB checked after a primary miss, or banked associativity. The indexing and storage are explicit in [zap_defines.svh:44](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_defines.svh:44) and [zap_tlb.sv:274](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_tlb.sv:274).
   If 75–100% of phase (c)’s 23% SDRAM-pending time disappears and was critical, speedup is \(1/(1-0.23×[0.75,1])=1.21–1.30×\), plus walker/restart overhead saved. The log’s approximately 35-clock walk every 78 clocks suggests **1.81× locally**, but only if aligned measurements validate that attribution. Phase (a)’s gain is unknown; zero if translation is disabled.
   Expansion adds under 1 KB of payload; budget roughly 0–4 additional M10Ks and hundreds of ALMs, depending on mapping. Associative selection threatens the already-critical TLB path; serial victim lookup avoids widening the primary-hit path.
   Verify alternating colliding pages, replacement, invalidation, FCSE, permissions and faults. Recheck DACR on every hit; retain domain/AP metadata, not cached permission decisions ([zap_tlb_check.sv:124](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_tlb_check.sv:124)).
2. **Make the I-cache two-way; establish the actual eviction causes first.**
   VA zero and 0x50320000 collide in the 16 KB I-cache. However, they explain only matching offsets, not automatically all 73 refetched lines. Record fill VA, displaced tag and invalidation cause. A TLB miss does not itself invalidate this virtually tagged cache.
   Keeping 16 KB total, two ways remove pairwise conflicts. If this eliminates 50–75% of flash waiting, gains are \(1/(1-0.25×[0.5,0.75])=1.14–1.23×\) in (a), and \(1/(1-0.32×[0.5,0.75])=1.19–1.32×\) in (c)—only where misses are conflicts.
   Data capacity stays unchanged; allow several M10Ks for packing/tag overhead and hundreds of ALMs for comparison, selection and replacement. Register selection rather than extending critical forwarding paths.
   Verify colliding instruction streams and self-modifying-code maintenance. Defer D-cache associativity until measured: it adds dirty-victim and index-maintenance complexity. Also, the current **8 KB D-cache index is VA[12:4]**, not [13:4] ([zap_defines.svh:113](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_defines.svh:113)).
3. **Specialize CP15 maintenance instead of draining/refetching universally.**
   D-only maintenance need not discard instruction prefetch. Preserve ordering against older data accesses, then optimize clean/invalid-line handling; consider a short maintenance queue only with correct completion barriers. Current code drains all older instructions, always refetches, and spends four states awaiting maintenance RAM data ([zap_core.sv:591](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_core.sv:591), [zap_predecode_main.sv:625](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_predecode_main.sv:625), [zap_cache_tag_ram.sv:429](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_cache_tag_ram.sv:429)).
   Reducing the **average** 38 clocks to an illustrative 8 saves \(0.02×30=0.60\) CPI: **1.05×** at 12.5 CPI, **1.03×** in the local window. Phase (a) reaches 1.06× only with the same maintenance frequency. Dirty writeback still costs time.
   Budget hundreds of ALMs and 0–1 M10K. Ordering risk exceeds storage cost. Verify dirty/clean/invalid lines, both TI ways, DMA visibility, exceptions, and domain/MMU changes. Crucially, c7,c14,2 currently requests **D-only** maintenance; it does not flush I-cache ([zap_cp15_cb.sv:664](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_cp15_cb.sv:664)).
4. **Reduce residual flash-path latency, then reconsider SDRAM policy.**
   Enlarge the physical flash cache to 64–128 KB and provide a registered word/line read-array path through the NOR model. Its existing two-clock hit is at the **halfword backend**, not the CPU port; the bridge still assembles halfwords ([ddram_flash.sv:8](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/mem/ddram_flash.sv:8), [upp_bus.sv:34](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/upp/upp_bus.sv:34)).
   Halving flash waiting alone gives **1.14× (a), 1.19× (c)**. Recalculate using residual waiting after item 2; these savings overlap. Budget approximately 60–140 additional M10Ks and hundreds of ALMs, with moderate timing risk.
   Verify array/status mode transitions, toggling status reads, programming, erasure, loader invalidation and saved-image consistency. Never bypass NOR semantics.
   Open-row SDRAM or a coherent physical descriptor cache is secondary: even halving *all* SDRAM waiting gives only **1.12× (a), 1.13× (c)** before overlap. Descriptor caches require write coherence; avoiding walks is preferable to accelerating recurring ones.
5. **Add a CPU write buffer only after measuring exposed store stalls.**
   There is an important discrepancy: although ZAP waits for acknowledgement, **the bridge already posts SDRAM writes** ([upp_bus.sv:451](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/upp/upp_bus.sv:451)). Nine controller clocks are not nine exposed CPU clocks.
   Even charging every reported write nine clocks gives \(0.11×6M×9/104M=5.7\%\), or **1.06×** maximum from hiding that component in (c); likely less. Phase (a) needs its own store-stall count.
   A 4–8-entry queue needs under 128 bytes, hundreds of ALMs and 0–1 M10K. Verify forwarding, byte enables, ordering, bufferability, aborts, drain and DMA visibility. MMIO must retain required ordering.
6. **Fix return prediction cheaply; defer pipeline reconstruction.**
   The MOV PC,LR return pattern incorrectly matches Rm=15 rather than 14 ([zap_predecode_main.sv:504](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/cpu/zap/zap_predecode_main.sv:504)). Correcting it is essentially resource-neutral.
   One rescued return per 40 instructions saves \((12-3)/40=0.225\) CPI: approximately **1.02× (a/c)** or **1.05× loop throughput (b)**, conditional on actual mispredictions. It does not shorten timer waits. Verify nested/conditional returns, ARM/Thumb transitions and exceptions. Eighteen-cycle exception entry at 25 SWIs/1000 contributes only **0.45 CPI**; it cannot explain 12.5 CPI alone.
The **48-second wait most likely reflects blocked startup services awaiting timeout/retry completion**, with Bluetooth/CBUS the leading suspect—not insufficient execution speed. Service 3/id 13 returns without sleeping ([nos_stub.S:146](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/model/nos-stub/nos_stub.S:146)); unimplemented CBUS devices largely behave as register storage ([upp_cbus.sv:49](/Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer/rtl/upp/upp_cbus.sv:49)). Correlate timestamped Bluetooth messages, blocked-thread wait objects, timer expiries, CBUS transactions and wakeups. Verify the kernel’s 64 Hz tick. Adding WFI reduces spinning, not timeout duration; skipping retries or inventing successful responses violates the constraint.
Before implementation, align trace windows and collect mutually attributable stalls. Afterwards, run directed correctness checks, firmware comparisons and all-corner timing on the exact generated netlist, leaving `sys/` untouched. CPU optimization alone cannot achieve a 20–30-second boot while a genuine 48-second wait remains.
