## Cut 1 — lock summaries on the update edge

The cache FSM now registers `o_lock_any <= |lock_nxt` and
`o_lock_owner <= lock_nxt[i_lock_owner_next]` beside `lock_ff <= lock_nxt`.
The registers are bounded against retiming. V2 sends the next W destination:
M's destination on RUN advance, RELOAD1 and quick reload, otherwise the
held W destination. Reset selects register zero, matching W's reset record.
A flush clears W's validity but does not change its destination independently
of that existing transfer rule. ZAP does not consume either summary.

V2 completion and its CP15 admission use these registered bits instead of
the live 64-bit reductions. Completion, retirement and reload still occur
on exactly the old edges. Normal-return readiness continues to inspect the
post-acceptance lock for its registered return tag: a miss ACK is not data.

`sim/cpu/arm9v2/lock_summary.py` extracts the actual capture expressions
from the cache FSM and proves their invariant inductively with Yosys. Its
unconstrained next bitmap covers multiple locks, simultaneous clear/install,
same-register replacement and every owner change without a single-owner
assumption. The proof and model are saved beside the fit evidence. Runtime
assertions additionally compare the summaries with the actual lock bitmap
and W one-hot owner on every edge. The full 267-run V2 source suite passes
with these assertions and every cycle/instruction pair equal to S9.

Generated-netlist acceptance: all three configurations pass 267/267 and
69/69 QEMU comparisons; all 85 windows match in both modes at all three
latencies, including raw 3/4/5. All four CPU/debug lint combinations and
`make cpu-gen-check` pass. See `arm9v2/s10-cut1-validation.json`.

Cut 1 fits at **-2.555 ns hot / -2.400 ns cold, 16,267 ALMs**. Against
the new-settings baseline, hot improves 0.123 ns, cold regresses 0.078 ns,
and area falls 294 ALMs. The global leader is now M's address through the
selected check/FCSE path into D-TLB `o_hit_ok`. Lock-to-completion reduction
no longer leads A's next-match capture. The new lock-owner capture itself
is -1.776 ns (9 levels, 10.704 ns); it is measured, not assumed free.
