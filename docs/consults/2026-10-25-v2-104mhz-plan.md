# V2 at 104 MHz: the plan (2026-10-25, no RTL changes)

Status: a plan, not a result. Nothing here has been built or fitted. The
numbers marked *estimate* are engineering estimates; the ones marked
*measured* cite their source.

## 0. Where we start, and what "done" means

**Measured.**
- **S12's integrated CPU/cache trial:** seed 2, D16, prediction on.
  - Setup slack is −2.951 ns at slow 100 C; hold is positive at all
    corners.
  - Area: 13,262 ALMs and 123 RAM blocks.
  - Sources: `docs/consults/2026-10-25-codex-arm9v2-s12.md:167-176`, and
    the endpoint table at `:199-225`.
- **Trend:** S9 to S11 implemented every zero-cycle cut of the eight-cut
  consult that applied (lock summaries, parallel producer matches,
  translation descriptors, short-RAM descriptors, banked metadata, data-
  ready grants, RF owner classes). The slack stayed between −2.47 and
  −2.98 ns throughout (`s10.md:34-47`, `s11.md:323-333`). Zero-cycle work
  on this shape is exhausted. This is the "probably not with the present
  stage boundaries" verdict of the back-on-track consult (master
  `docs/consults/2026-10-25-codex-backontrack-consult.md:90-107`).
- **In play at 78 MHz, per 104 M clocks:** 41-44 M instr/s (Sonic 44.0,
  Asphalt 41.5, Tony Hawk 41.8, fix-2 board runs), so CPI ≈ 2.36-2.51.
  The goal of 47 M/s needs CPI ≤ 2.21.
- **Fix 3 (`fe65a2d`, the D-TLB walks):** not yet on the board. From the
  whole-core cost (20.5 clocks per avoided walk) and the board's
  100-140 K excess D-beats per 104 M clocks, it should give **+2-3 %**
  (*estimate*, §5 of `2026-10-25-v2-msr-lock-fix.md`).

**The target.** The user's gate is "meets timing at 104 MHz". The
shipping ZAP build is −0.58 ns in the same wrapper and runs reliably on
the board, which tolerates about −0.8 ns whole-device.
- I take the acceptance target to be a **whole-device slow-corner slack
  of at least −0.6 ns** (ZAP's own), with positive hold.
- `docs/ARM9V2.md:49-58` says the whole device "must actually close". The
  lead has to say which of the two applies: −0.6 ns or 0.
- **The CPU-trial target that follows:** whole-device slack has been 0.3
  to 1.0 ns worse than the CPU trial (the planning table in
  `2026-10-24-codex-v2-timing-consult.md:72-75`). So the trial needs
  about **−0.3 ns or better, median of seeds 2/3/4**, and **every endpoint
  family at −0.4 ns or better**. That is 2.6 ns of improvement on the
  leader, and roughly 1-2.6 ns on **fifteen** families, not four.

**Scepticism first.** The four families the brief names do not cover the
second tier. Even with all four gone, the trial would lead at about
−2.0 ns: `metadata_descriptor` −2.033, `rf` −1.981, `backend` and
`execute` −1.967, `backend_skid` −1.772, `decode_payload` −1.754,
`execute_late` −1.742, `dtlb` −1.728, `decode_grants` −1.722, `decode_d1`
−1.632, `fetch` −1.448, `locks` −1.430 (`s12.md:199-225`).

Those families share six root causes, A-F below. Every one has to be
cut. A plan that cuts only A-C will move the leader and not the slack,
as S9-S11 did.

## 1. The boundary changes, family by family

Today's pipeline (`docs/ARM9V2.md:84-118`):

```
F1  F2  D1  D2  S              A                         M                      W
PC  I$  Thm RF  fwd/rotate     late-bypass + ALU         D$/TLB compare,        commit, RF write,
BTB tag dec haz (registered    -> flags, ADDRESS ------> format, short word --> late pickup source
            producers)             (comb.)  launches D$/TLB RAM read           (tr_cache_words)
```

Scalar hit windows, counting the two marker clocks: **3/4/5** for an
independent instruction, a late consumer (unshifted operand, picked up
at A) and an early consumer (shifted or base operand, needed at S). The
load-to-consumer spacing is therefore 1/2/3 (`docs/ARM9V2.md:113-118`).

### A. The A-stage address drives the cache and TLB RAMs combinationally

**Families:**
- `metadata` −2.951: `a[43]` → dirty bank address;
- `tlb_lookup` −2.666: `a[43]` → `g_short.read_valid`;
- `metadata_descriptor` −2.033;
- `short_collision` −1.262.

**The path.** A's late bypass (`arm9v2_execute.sv:241-245`) feeds the
ALU, which produces the address (`:332`, `:343`
`o_address=a.ins[24]?alu_result:left_a`). That becomes `a_data_address`,
then `lookup_address` and `o_data_wb_adr_nxt` (`arm9v2_core.sv:662-665`).
These drive the D-cache tag, data and metadata RAM addresses and the TLB
RAM in the same clock (`zap_cache_tag_ram.sv:579`, `zap_ram_simple.sv:55`,
`zap_mem_inv_block.sv` `g_short`).

**Preferred cut: A1, an address generator in S (an "S-AGU").** No new
stage. S already selects registered operands for every load or store
(the base, and an immediate or unshifted register offset). Add a
dedicated 32-bit adder after S's selection, in parallel with the rotator,
and register the effective address at the S/A edge. A then launches the
D-cache and TLB reads from that **register**, removing the bypass, ALU
and address-mux chain from all four paths.
- **Writeback bases:** forward the S-AGU's own registered result as the
  next S-AGU base, so `ldr r1,[r0],#4; ldr r2,[r0],#4` chains at full
  rate.
- **Scaled-register offsets** (`[Rn, Rm, LSL #n]`) take the existing
  rotator and are summed one clock later: the record holds in S, so +1.
- **A base produced by the instruction immediately ahead** (an ALU result
  at A while the load is in S) cannot be in S's registered producers. The
  load holds one clock in S: +1.

```
F1  F2  D1  D2  S                   A                  M        W
                fwd/rotate          ALU, flags         (same)   (same)
                + AGU adder ---reg--> registered address
                                   launches D$/TLB RAM read
```

- **Windows:** 3/4/5 unchanged for every load whose base is ready at S.
  +1 for an ALU-produced base used immediately, and for scaled-register
  offsets.
- **Predictor and RAS:** unaffected; no stage is added.
- **New S path:** selection, then the adder, then a register. That is
  about as deep as selection, rotator, register, which S11 brought to
  `execute` −1.967 / `decode_grants` −1.722. **This is the risk of A1.**
  If the adder after selection fits worse than the rotator, A1 fails.
- **Side effect:** the lookup and check addresses become the same
  registered address, one clock apart. That removes the structural cause
  of fix 3's stale-lookup class (the TLB RAM's lookup is no longer A's
  speculative value).

**Fallback: A2, an address-launch stage L.**
`F1 F2 D1 D2 S A L M W`, as proposed in
`2026-10-24-codex-v2-timing-consult.md:81-93`.

```
F1  F2  D1  D2  S        A                L                    M           W
                         ALU -> reg addr  launches D$/TLB      compare,    commit
                         (and result)     RAM read             format
```

- **Windows:** **3/5/6** (late spacing 3, early 4).
- **Redirects:** W-resolved ones (loads to PC, W RAS validation) cost one
  more clock; A-resolved branches cost nothing extra.
- **RAS:** the checkpoint must cover one more in-flight record. Precise
  exceptions must kill one more record. Every miss and uncached request
  launches a clock later.

**Cycle cost, A1 (*estimate*).** Measured board rates per 104 M clocks
are about 42 M instructions with roughly 18 % loads and stores. The
fraction of memory instructions whose base comes from the immediately
preceding ALU instruction, or that use a scaled-register offset, is
**not measured**; guess 5-10 %. That gives 0.4-0.8 M extra clocks,
**+0.4-0.8 %**.

**Cycle cost, A2 (*estimate*).** Every late or early load dependency
pays +1, and consumers at distance 2 (late) or 3 (early) newly pay +1.
The busy firmware phase from EFile to EStart spends 8.6 % of its clocks
on dependency (4.60 M of 53.47 M, `s13.md:137-140`). Play is not
measured. Bracket: **+3 to +8 %**, plus +0.07 % for W redirects (board:
40-62 K W flushes per 104 M clocks).

### B. The cache's load word enters A's late bypass, then the ALU and flags

**Families:**
- `backend_m` −2.564: `tr_cache_words[4]` → `m[427]`, which is
  `next_flags[30]` (Z) (`s11.md:340-344`);
- `backend_skid` −1.772;
- `execute_late` −1.742, which is partly cache state → late operand.

**The path.** The registered short word from W is selected into A's late
bypass (`arm9v2_execute.sv:241-245`, the `i_mw_value` lanes), passes the
adder and logic network, then Z reduction (`:295` `next_flags`), into M's
record. A1 and A2 do not touch it (`s11.md:340-344`).

Three steps, cheapest first; each is gated on the family's own slack:

1. **B1, parallel arithmetic zero (zero cycles).** Z from the identity
   `(a+b+cin)==0` iff `(a^b)=={(a[30:0]|b[30:0]),cin}`, which needs no
   carry chain. S11 checked the identity with SAT
   (`s11-zero-flag-identity.{v,log}`); its estimate is 0.7-1.3 ns on this
   family (`s11.md:367-373`). Logic-op Z, and N/C/V, need their own path
   checks.
2. **B2, flag-setting late consumers of a load take the early path.**
   Typical code is `ldr; cmp/tst/...s`. The consumer holds one more
   clock, so the late window is 4→5 **for flag-setting consumers only**.
   The result lane keeps its late pickup.
   - Cycle cost (*estimate*): 10-20 % of loads have an immediate
     flag-setting consumer, so **+0.4-0.9 %**.
3. **B3, no late pickup of load words.** ALU results from M keep their
   late pickup; load words do not. Windows become **3/5/5**, or **3/6/6**
   with A2. This removes the W→A load bypass entirely, which also removes
   `backend_skid`.
   - Cycle cost (*estimate*): every late consumer within distance 2 pays
     +1, so **+2.5-5 %**.
   - This is the fallback listed in `timing-consult.md:99`.

### C. The short-port read: RAM, then way select, then format, then the registered word

**Family:** `short_response` −2.226: `g_short_ram.rd_addr_q[7]` →
`tr_cache_words[8]`.

**The path.** The MLAB read feeds hit/way selection, then `formatted`
(`zap_dcache.sv:433-470`; `ld_transform` handles byte, half, signed and
rotated loads, and collision merges), then the capture. A, B, D and E do
not touch it.

1. **C1, format each way in parallel, select the way last (zero
   cycles).** Run the byte-lane, sign and rotation logic per way on its
   RAM output and the registered format. The hit and way decision, which
   arrives last, selects finished words. The store-collision merge
   (`store_collision ? ld_transform(store_word, …)`) also becomes a
   parallel candidate.
   - Expected local gain (*estimate*): 0.8-1.5 ns.
   - Area: about 2 × 32 bits of formatter.
2. **C2 (fallback), sub-word loads format in W.** Aligned word loads take
   the fast path (way select only). LDRB, LDRH, LDRSB, LDRSH and
   unaligned LDR register the raw word and format in W.
   - Their consumers pay +1: windows 3/5/6 for sub-word loads, 3/4/5 for
     words. That matches ARM9TDMI's own extra clock for byte/half loads
     (`docs/ARM9V2.md:235`).
   - Cycle cost (*estimate*): unknown sub-word share. Game pixel and
     sprite code is byte/half-heavy, so assume 20-40 % of loads,
     **+1.5-3.5 %**.

### D. D-cache FSM state and completion drive core control combinationally

**Families:**
- `rf` −1.981: `state_ff[2]` → RF `mem`;
- `backend` −1.967: `state_ff[2]` → `wb_flush`;
- `decode_grants` −1.722: `state_ff[2]` → `o_forward_grant`;
- `execute_late` −1.742: `state_ff[5]` → `a[42]`;
- `locks` −1.430: `o_fsr` → `o_lock_owner`;
- `decode_matches` −0.979.

**The path.** The cache FSM state goes through `request_done` to
`complete_class` and `done` (`arm9v2_core.sv:477-491`). From there it
reaches the RF write-port selection, W's flush and redirect, S's
forwarding grants, A's late operand and the lock-owner query, all in the
same clock. S10 and S11's zero-cycle summaries shortened each cone but
left the completion → selection chain (`s10.md:302-313`).

**D1, a registered completion contract.** For every **non-short**
completion (miss fill end, uncached access, store-fill acceptance,
CP15/maintenance end, SWP phase, RETRY/RELOAD ends), the cache presents a
**registered** "W's request completes" status, carrying its owner tag.
The core consumes it one clock later.
- **Short hits are unchanged.** They already complete on the cache's
  registered short response, so 3/4/5 holds.
- **Cost:** +1 clock per non-short completion.
- **Ownership:** the lock install and clear edge moves with completion.
  This is exactly the transition class behind fix 1, fix 2 and the
  MSR-lock bug, so §3's ownership tests are mandatory before any fit is
  believed.
- **Cycle cost (*estimate*):** the board shows ld-fill + st-fill +
  uncached ≈ 1.0-1.3 M per 104 M clocks in play, so **+1.0-1.3 %**, less
  whatever already overlaps fill latency.

### E. Front end: queue, D1, D2 and fetch

**Families:**
- `decode_payload` −1.754: `a[41]` → `d2[143]`, an A event into D2's
  payload capture;
- `decode_d1` −1.632: `head_select` → `d1`;
- `fetch` −1.448: `read_pc` → RAS `stack`;
- `icache` −1.197: `read_pc` → I-RAM address.

All of these have zero-cycle candidates that are already designed but not
built (`s11.md:365-366`):
- capture the Thumb halfword and bounded major-decode hints at queue push
  (0.6-1.2 ns);
- compute banked destination candidates in parallel with D1
  classification (0.5-1.0 ns);
- make A's kill of D2 a validity/epoch change, not a payload-mux select,
  as `docs/ARM9V2.md:97-98` requires but `decode_payload` shows is not
  the case;
- register the RAS push/pop decision, so the pointer update leaves the
  PC path.

**Cycle cost:** 0 if they fit. The fallback is +1 lost slot on
unpredicted D2 direct redirects (3→4). The board shows A flushes of
220-296 K per 104 M clocks; D2 redirects are fewer, so the cost is at
most **+0.3 %**.

### F. Register-file write-through, D-TLB permission and the rest

- **`execute` −1.967** (`write_through_data[63]` → `a[138]`): D2's
  registered RF write-through (`arm9v2_decode.sv:574-581`) feeds S's
  operand capture. The path still has to be inspected. Two candidates:
  select the write-through into D2's own output register, or replace it
  with a W′ (previous-W) record in S's producer set. The second adds a
  lane to S, so measure it against A1's adder. Cost: 0.
- **`dtlb` −1.728** (TLB RAM port → `o_hit_ok`) and `tlb_metadata`
  −0.860: `o_hit_ok` qualifies only background write-back store hits
  (`zap_tlb_check.sv` comment at `o_hit_ok`). Registering it one clock
  later for stores costs nothing visible, apart from same-line
  store→load windows (re-measure the 4 block-tail windows). Another
  option: precompute resolved AP bits per entry at install, leaving the
  DACR domain select and an AND. Cost: about 0.

**In play:**
- **Preferred path:** 42.5 M/s × 1/1.024, plus fix 3's +2-3 %, gives
  about **42-43 M/s at 104 MHz**. That meets the user's "near 41-44" and

## 2. Order, gates and lanes

Each step is a separately fittable source state. Its gate is **its
families' own slack** from the per-family endpoint queries
(`s12-final-seed2/families.json`), not the global worst. Each step must
also pass:
- **(i)** the full suites in five configurations (ZAP D8 and D16; V2 D8,
  D16 and predictor off);
- **(ii)** QEMU 73/73;
- **(iii)** an updated window table, with every changed window
  documented (no silent retargeting, `docs/ARM9V2.md:270-272`);
- **(iv)** ZAP cycle-identity;
- **(v)** whole-core firmware milestones (first exec, EFile, EStart) with
  the clock change recorded.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Profile 2-3 guest seconds of play from snapshots of Sonic and Asphalt. | Projected preferred-path CPI ≤ 2.54 (41 M/s). Otherwise stop before any RTL. | — | Mac + 1 server |
| 1 | A1 S-AGU | metadata, tlb_lookup, metadata_descriptor, short_collision ≥ −0.4; S families (execute, decode_grants) not worse than −1.9 | 0 | server 1 |
| 2 | C1 per-way formatting | short_response ≥ −0.4 | 0 | server 2 |
| 3 | E front-end cuts | decode_payload, decode_d1, fetch, icache ≥ −0.4 | 0 | server 3 |
| 4 | B1 parallel zero, then B2 if needed | backend_m, backend_skid ≥ −0.4 (after B2) | 0 | server 4 |

Steps 1-4 touch disjoint logic: the S stage, the cache formatter, the
front end and the ALU flags. They can be built on four branches and
fitted in parallel on the four server lanes (about 1 h per fit), with
the Mac running suites and QEMU. Their windows interact only through
B2/B3 versus A2. Fallbacks are decided per family after its own fit:
- **A2** only if A1's S path fails its gate;
- **B3** only if B1+B2 leave `backend_m` below −1.0;
- **C2** only if C1 leaves `short_response` below −1.0.

Every fallback taken is re-costed with step 0's histograms before it is
merged.

**Stop rule** (`docs/ARM9V2.md:50`): if step 7's integrated median is
below **−1.5 ns** with all preferred cuts in, stop this shape. Do not
search seeds, and report the remaining chains. If it is between −1.5 and
−0.3, take the fallbacks for the leading families only, and re-cost
cycles before the whole-device build.

## 3. Verification

**Must stay green at every step.**
- The suites at five latencies (0:0, 0:3, 2:9, 8:12, 16:40), the 85+4
  windows (expected values updated only where documented), QEMU 73/73,
  and the four lint configurations.
- `cpu-gen-check`, and ZAP cycle-identity in D8 and D16.
- The ownership directed tests of this branch:
  - `arm9_msr_lock`, `arm9_swi_lock`, `arm9_store_reload`;
  - `arm9_install_edge`, `arm9_fetch_redirect`, `arm9_dep_waw`;
  - `arm9_unc_chain`, `arm9_unc_copy`;
  - `arm9_tlb_walks`, `arm9_tlb_stale`.
- The whole-core `flashcopy` (memory side, `--check-flash`).

**New tests, per boundary.**
- **A1:**
  - loads and stores whose base comes from the instruction at distance
    1, 2 and 3, by producer class (ALU, load, multiply, writeback chain);
  - scaled versus unscaled offsets, pre- and post-indexed, writeback,
    LDM/STM base;
  - lookup and check alignment, asserted on every edge (the TLB check
    address equals the registered S-AGU address of the same record);
  - a hold in A with an S-AGU address queued;
  - aborts on the registered address, including base restoration.
- **A2, if taken:** one more in-flight record through flush, abort,
  IRQ/FIQ, CP15 fences and RAS repair; W-resolved PC loads; and the
  3/5/6 windows.
- **B:** flag-setting versus non-setting consumers at distances 1-3;
  a conditional consumer; a condition-failed producer exposing an older
  writer; MRS after a load-fed `S` instruction.
- **C:** every LDR/LDRB/LDRH/LDRSB/LDRSH/unaligned load × both ways × a
  store collision in the same set × fill installation. For C2, also each
  sub-word form against consumers at distances 1-3.
- **D:** non-short completions (miss, dirty victim, uncached, SWP, CP15
  clean, RETRY/RELOAD), each with:
  - a reader at distances 1-3;
  - a younger same-register writer (the fix-1/fix-2 class);
  - an MSR/SWI serialiser before it (the MSR-lock class);
  - IRQ arrival on the completion edge.

  Assert every edge: an accepted pending load keeps its destination and
  age until completion; a reader gets the newest older writer; an older
  return never supersedes a younger write. These are the independent
  ownership assertions from `backontrack-consult.md:76-80`.
- **E:** Thumb pairs across the queue boundary, LDM/STM lists, faulted
  fetch, A kill on the same clock as D2 capture, RAS overflow, underflow
  and repair.

**Firmware.**
- **Whole-core bench at 104 MHz nominal:** first exec, EFile, EStart and
  4.5 guest seconds with no D/P/U fault, at every step. Record the clock
  change against S12's 337,570,706 / 375,876,178 / 429,348,643.
- **After steps 5 and 7:** the snapshot-based play profile (step 0's
  method) for the cycle projection.

**Board.**
- **At 78 MHz, after step 7:** the integrated source, in matched scenes
  (the 20-minute runs). Report 104 × Δinstructions / Δclocks with a ZAP
  control.
- **At 104 MHz:** only after step 8's whole-device fit, and only once
  gate 1 (no panic) is shown on the 78 MHz board with the same RTL apart
  from timing.

## 4. Risks and fallbacks

1. **A1's S path does not fit.** The adder after S's selection becomes the
   new leader. Fall back to A2 (+3-8 % cycles). Measure this first: it
   decides whether the preferred total (+2-3 %) or the fallback total
   holds.
2. **The second tier resists.** D and F are each 9-10-level control
   cones with fanout (`s10.md:290-313`). If D1 leaves `rf`/`backend`
   below −1.0, the next lever is cycle-costly: registered W redirects
   (+1 on A/W flushes, about +0.5 %).
3. **Correctness regressions.** D1 moves the lock install and clear edge,
   which is the bug class this branch fixed three times. Gate 1 (the
   panic) is not yet shown fixed on the board (fix 3 waits). No timing
   step goes to the board before gate 1 holds on the 78 MHz source.
4. **The whole-device penalty is worse than 1 ns.** It is not measured
   for V2. Step 8 could miss with a passing trial. Budget one more pass
   of local placement (loose region around the backend and D-cache,
   `timing-consult.md:64`); not a seed search.
5. **Area and RAM.** C1 duplicates a formatter, A1 adds an adder, and B2
   and D1 add registers. The device is at 539/553 M10K with D16. D8 is
   the area fallback, at 2-2.6 % of cycles (D16's measured gain).
6. **The performance gate fails even if timing closes.** The preferred
   path lands at about 42-43 M/s, under 47. Getting closer needs data-wait
   work: the board shows 20-28 % data stalls in play, and S13 shows V2's
   data wait at 2× ZAP's in boot (`s13.md:184`). That is a separate
   track and should not be mixed into the timing steps.

**A reduced feature set that should close, ranked by cost per ns
(*estimates*).**
- **B3 (no load-word late pickup):** windows 3/5/5, +2.5-5 %. Removes B's
  three families.
- **C2 (sub-word format in W):** +1.5-3.5 %. Removes `short_response`.
- **A2 (L stage) instead of A1:** +3-8 %.
- **Together:** about +8-17 % cycles, giving 36-39 M/s at 104 MHz.
