## What changed

All three are zero-cycle and on for ARM9 only. ZAP is unchanged. Each
is checked in simulation against the serial form on every clock.

**`WAY_FORMAT`** (`zap_dcache.sv`): the plan's C1.
- Each way's word, and its store-collision merge, is formatted before
  the hit chooses a way. The formatting is the byte or halfword select,
  the sign and the rotation.
- The hit, the last-arriving term, drives only the final 2:1 select.
- The C2 fallback (sub-word loads formatted in W, +1 clock) is not
  needed.

**`REG_COLLISION`** (`zap_cache_tag_ram.sv`).
- **What the trial showed:** C's leader did not start at the tag
  compare. It started at the short RAMs' read/write set collision,
  `rd_addr_q == wr_addr_q`. That compare follows the address registers
  and drives about 280 byte and tag bypass selects.
- **The change:** the compare is made before the edge that loads both
  registers (`collision_q`), so the selects come from one register.

**`W_ROTATE`** (`arm9v2_execute.sv`): item 2, the device leader at −2.31.
- **What the shifter does to a load word there:** nothing specific to
  the load. The rotator applies the *consumer's* operand shift, an
  immediate LSL/LSR/ASR/ROR/RRX or amount 0. Sub-word and unaligned
  loads are already formatted by the cache before `tr_cache_words`.
- **Why it is on every operand-1 path:** an unshifted operand also
  passes the rotator at amount 0 into `a.right`. So every consumer that
  is in S while the load is in W is on the path: distance 3 for any
  consumer, and distance 1-2 for an early (shifted) one after its stall.
- **The cut:** W's lane 0, the forward network's last input, gets a
  rotator of its own with S's shift controls. The main rotator's input
  excludes that lane, and the grant selects the finished value.

## The test

**`arm9_short_format_rtl`.**
- **The loads:** every load form (LDR at byte offsets 0-3, LDRB/LDRSB
  at 0-3, LDRH/LDRSH at 0 and 2) hits a line in way 0, then in way 1.
- **The readers:** a late ALU operand, and ROR, LSL with its carry, and
  ASR, at distances 1 and 3.
- **The stores:** a word, byte or halfword store to the same word at
  distance 1 or 2; a store to the set's other way; and a store followed
  by 1-3 loads of another set.
- **The references:** every expected value is an assembly-time
  constant built with MOV/ORR. A first version compared against an
  uncached reload, and a way-swap mutant corrupted both the result and
  the reference.

**Mutants** (each fails the test at 0:0/0:3/2:9):
- the way select inverted;
- a store collision matched in the wrong way;
- the set collision registered a clock late, or ignored;
- S's W-lane rotator fed the wrong lane.
