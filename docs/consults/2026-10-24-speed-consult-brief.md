THE GOAL AND WHERE WE ARE
- The goal is the phone's speed in play: the real N-Gage (an ARM9-class core at 104 MHz, OMAP-like UPP_WD2 board) delivers about 45-50 M instructions/s in games once cache misses are counted; our yardstick is 47 M/s, from Asphalt Urban GT's 21 fps cap times its 2.3 M instructions a frame. Accuracy may bend to reach it (the user's words).
- Today, on the board (the MiSTer's Cyclone V 5CSEBA6U23I7, the CPU on the phone's 104 MHz clock): 31 M instructions/s in play at CPI 3.3 (Sonic N 31.1, Asphalt 31.7, Tony Hawk 30.6), frame rates Sonic 16, Asphalt 13 of 21, Tony Hawk 12. That is 60-65 % of the phone. Measured today after the latest change (the dirty victim's write-back deferred behind the fill: +4 to +6 % in play; before it 29.4-30.3). The log of all measurements: docs/PROGRESS-LOG.md (newest at the end; the 2026-10-22 "game speed on the hardware" entry explains the counters, the 2026-10-23 entries the experiments).
- The CPU is ZAP (rtl/cpu/zap/, ARM925T configuration, arm925t_gen.v generated from it), a 32-bit ARMv5-class core: 16 KB two-way I-cache, 8 KB two-way D-cache (VIVT, 16-byte lines), TLBs, CP15, a Wishbone merger to a 32-bit bus; hit-under-miss in the D-cache; the fill of a line from the MiSTer's SDRAM takes 12-15 clocks; the firmware is the real Symbian 6.1 / EKA1 ROM (kernel, drivers, file system, everything), not a replacement kernel. The stripped core uses 36,657 of 41,910 ALMs (87 %), 487 M10K, 43 DSPs; its slack is -0.87 ns at the best seed (timing closure is paused while the CPU work lands; the rule is "not drastically worse").
- The per-second counters in play (Sonic N, a debug build; tools/mister_dbg.py decodes them; NGage.sv around line 1135 lists them; zap_core.sv line 1775 defines the stall taps): stalls data 10 % (D-cache not ready), code 48 % (I-cache not ready: miss or fill), fetch FIFO empty 12 %, issue 35 % (operand interlocks), shifter 17 %, decode 7 %; flushes from the ALU 390 K/s and from writeback 150 K/s; 33 M uops for 31 M instructions; 130 K replays/s; SDRAM wait 19 % of clocks; D-cache events per second about 310 K load fills, 330 K store fills, 300 K write-backs, 300 K uncached accesses. The taps overlap (a clock can count in several). I-cache miss counts are not instrumented.
- Memory-side experiments on the board, all measured (docs/PROGRESS-LOG.md 2026-10-23): a 32 KB I-cache +1 % (NGAGE_ICACHE_32K), a 16 KB D-cache +1-3 % (NGAGE_DCACHE_16K), I-cache next-line prefetch 0 % (NGAGE_ICACHE_PREFETCH), hit-under-miss +3 %, the deferred write-back +4-6 %, a 4 KB MMC chunk path (loads are CPU-bound). An estimate from the stall shares put ZAP's ceiling at 36-38 M/s with every memory-side lever pulled. A write buffer in the merger was estimated at 5-8 % and parked. A cycle-identical ZAP timing round (nine changes, codex/timing2, -0.22 ns median) exists but is parked, because ZAP is not the future.

THE NEW CPU, ARM9 V2
- Our own eight-stage core, rtl/cpu/arm9v2/ on the branch codex/arm9v2 (worktree /Users/jacob/dev/fpga/mister/NGage_MiSTer-cx-arm9; design document docs/ARM9V2.md there; session reports docs/consults/2026-10-23-codex-arm9v2-s1.md to -s5.md there), stages F1 F2 D1 D2 S A M W, a BTB/predictor, a forwarding network with three registered producers (A, M, W) plus a background RF port, the same shared caches/TLBs/CP15/merger as ZAP (CORE=ARM9 selects it; ZAP stays the default). Status after five sessions: the whole CPU suite passes on V2 (261/261 runs, 68 QEMU references) in both predictor modes; the integrated CPU-with-caches fit is -2.1 ns at 104 MHz at seed 2 (ZAP in the same wrapper: -0.58 ns, 16,549 ALMs; V2 15,745 ALMs, its integer core about 8,260 ALMs); the load-use schedule is not done: a LDR followed by a dependent ADD costs 8 clocks (five extra; the target is three), so on the suite's random game-like tests V2 is level with ZAP (crand1 V2/ZAP 1.016 / 0.963 / 0.989 cycles at memory latencies 0:0 / 0:3 / 2:9), 7 % faster on the cache test, 11-13 % on the GBA proxies. Session 6 is running now: merge, three structural timing cuts, then the three-clock load pickup and the RAS checkpoint. The CPI the design aimed at was about 1.7 (a 35 % cycle reduction proxy); it is nowhere near yet.

THE EXISTENCE PROOF: THE ANALOGUE POCKET CORE
- plasticbugs/n-gage (source cloned read-only at /private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-jacob-dev-fpga-mister-NGage-MiSTer/a3d3155e-0d5b-4fb6-bc9d-97caf4499fda/scratchpad/pocket-src/n-gage: rtl/arm_*.sv about 2,100 lines, docs/core-design.md 1,474 lines, docs/hardware.md, METHODOLOGY.md; our reading notes in docs/consults/2026-10-23-plasticbugs-pocket-core.md). Their own ARMv4T core, eight stages, a 256-entry BTB answering in the fetch cycle, 64 KB I and D caches, about 5,000 ALMs, 80 MHz on its own PLL on a Cyclone V of the same family, lockstep-verified against Unicorn; CPI 1.7-1.9 in play with a 36-clock line fill (slower memory than ours). It runs only the firmware's user side on a small C kernel that replaces EKA1 (no real kernel, drivers, MMU page-table walking as the phone does it, flash, file system), so its instruction stream is not ours; its timing closure went six stages 61 MHz, eight stages 96 MHz CPU-only through seven rounds, then settled at 80 MHz for the whole core. Their gameplay-trace cache model: I 16->32 KB -5.8 %/-2.5 % cycles, store miss completing at the fill's launch -3.7 %/-5.3 % (ours already did), D 16->32 KB -2.7 %/-3.6 %, next-line D prefetch a loss; on their RTL I 64 KB +5.2 %/+2.4 %, BTB 512 +1.2 %/+0.6 %. No BTB costs 0.63-0.83 cycles per instruction on their pipeline; a fetch-cycle BTB 0.15-0.20, the same BTB a cycle late 0.26-0.34. Licence: none; read for insight, copy nothing.
