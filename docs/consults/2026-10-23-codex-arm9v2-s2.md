# ARM9V2 session 2: front-end timing and D1/D2

This report is updated at each milestone. **First milestone complete:** the
same-cycle front end now meets 104 MHz in all three component fits, median
worst-slow slack **+0.712 ns**, versus **-0.856 ns** in session 1. **D1/D2 is
the next milestone and is not included in these fits.** ZAP remains default;
`CORE="ARM9"` still selects the paused implementation. No V2 CPU or gameplay
performance result is implied.

## Front-end timing rounds

Section 1 of the requested main-worktree Antigravity consultation was read,
along with the session-1 worst paths for seeds 2–4. The old front end's 30
worst paths at each worst slow corner fed the next lookup's `read_valid`.
Seed 2 ran through the valid-array selection; seed 3 also exposed the
same-index collision comparison; seed 4 included the return-target choice.
Their worst data delays were 7.909 / 8.132 / 8.229 ns, six or seven logic
levels, with approximately -2.1 ns RAM-to-flop clock skew. Selecting the next
PC was followed by another address-dependent control calculation.

Round 1 moves both checks to registered lookup metadata. `read_valid` now
snapshots all 256 validity bits, with same-edge feedback applied. The bit
selection uses registered `read_pc`, in parallel with the RAM tag comparison.
Unlike reading live validity after the lookup, the snapshot cannot turn a
held miss into a hit when a later update changes that index. A new directed
test checks this case. The extra bitmap flops largely occupy spare ALM
register capacity.

The shared RAM's opt-in `REGISTER_COLLISION` captures read/write addresses,
write enable and data. Its collision comparison runs after the edge, beside
the RAM read response, and retains write-first data through held cycles.
Existing callers retain the default implementation. No D-cache source was
changed. This removes the late launch address -> collision compare -> flag
register path while keeping exactly the original lookup/collision latency.

Round 1's new worst path was RAM -> tag decision -> selected launch ->
sequential-PC increment. Round 2 computes the predicted and sequential
continuations independently, then selects the finished value. Explicit
`keep` wires prevent the two sums being folded into a shared adder after the
late decision. There is no additional pipeline stage.

The lookup-to-launch family still necessarily exists for a same-cycle BTB.
The avoidable control and arithmetic after it have been removed. A delayed
answer was not implemented or fitted: the external estimate of about 0.1 CPI
would mean about 4.5% more clocks at CPI 2.2, and its actual cost in V2 remains
unmeasured. Positive timing in all three seeds with unchanged cadence makes
retaining same-cycle prediction the supported choice at this milestone.

and source hashes are retained under `arm9v2/s2-frontend-r1-seed{2,3,4}` and

the exact fitted component. Remote directories are in each manifest. Jobs
ran one at a time, with the script checking the four-global/two-own limits.
No Quartus ran on the Mac; `--rm` removes completed containers.

## Consultation checklist and scope

- One common forwarding network with three registered producers remains the
  S/A design contract. D1/D2 will have raw RF reads and same-edge RF
  write-through only. A/M forwarding and background-load producer capture
  belong to the later execution/memory milestones.
- PC-relative targets belong in decode, comparisons use registered targets,
  and redirect events/payloads must be registers. No combinational execute
  event may drive fetch or decode admission.
- Fetch status only qualifies queue insertion/status. The front end has no
  cache-response bypass or Thumb decoder. D1 will own the one expansion.
- Block population count/first selection must precede RF read. Later list
  scans must terminate at registered register indices, not feed RF muxes in
  the same cycle.
- D-cache byte merge/format placement is outside this session and those files
  remain untouched. The registered-address M/W design, D-stage hazards and
  registered capacity must prevent live ACK/err2 propagating through S/A.

The consultation's claim of zero extra cost while retaining a two-clock
load-use was for the older five-stage proposal, and was not demonstrated by
its integrated timing. It does not override V2's explicit three-clock
registered-address load-use design. Likewise its two-clock predicted branch
row is superseded by V2's measured one-word-per-clock fetch cadence; complete
instruction timing still needs the backend. The structural recommendations
are adopted; unmeasured cycle guarantees are not treated as evidence.
