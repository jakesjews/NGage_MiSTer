- RTL `4993a1e` (`arm9v2_core/execute.sv`, `arm9_alu.sv`, `zap_dcache.sv`,

## What changed

Three parameters, default 1 in `arm9v2_core` and 0 in the modules. ZAP
is untouched: the cache FSM's new register drives nothing when
`SHORT_READ` is 0.

**`FREE_SELECT`: the data selects stop looking at W's hit and the
trust** (`arm9v2_execute.sv`, `arm9v2_core.sv`).
- **S's forward grant** is always the alternative D2 precomputed for a
  hit (`i_forward_grant[1]`). The other alternative is no longer used.
- **A's late bypass** selects by registered one-hot bits (`a.take`, the
  priority encoding of `a.late`, loaded on the same edges). It is a
  two-level AND-OR of the record's operand, M's and W's two lanes, and
  `block_base` for a later block transfer.
- **The core's W load word** loses its hit gate.
- **Why this is safe:** a source whose producer is a load without an
  accepted hit still waits. `s_wait` and `a_wait` keep `i_load_hit` and
  the trust. The word such a source selected is held as pending (S's
  `held`/`s_pending`, A's `a.pending`). The producer's hit, a newer
  producer or the return replaces it before it is used.
- **Checked in simulation** (`ifndef SYNTHESIS`):
  - every S or A operand that does not wait equals the old selector's
    word, with the old hit gate;
  - both grant alternatives match the old age and readiness rule;
  - `a.take` equals the encoding of `a.late`.

**`TRUST_FF`: the trust and W's hit candidate are registers of their
own.**
- **The trust:** the D-cache FSM loads `o_live_trust` with
  `(state_nxt[IDLE] || hum_slot_nxt) && !(o_fill_busy && !fill_busy_nxt)`.
  That is `i_data_short_live && !fill_ended`, computed a clock early from
  the same terms. The core asserts that the two are equal on every clock.
- **W's hit candidate:** `w_hit_candidate` holds `w.memory && !w_waited`,
  loaded from `w_next` and `w_waited`'s next value (asserted equal).
- **The result:** in the trial and on the device, `o_live_lookup` has no
  timing path to any CPU register.

**`FAST_Z`: Z without the carry chain** (`arm9_alu.sv`).
- **Arithmetic Z:** `(a^b) == {a[30:0]|b[30:0], cin}`, on the inverted
  operands. A zero sum needs every carry-in to equal `a^b`, and then
  every carry-out is `a|b`. This was checked exhaustively at 8 bits and
  on 200,000 random 32-bit cases, and S11 had already checked it with SAT.
- **Logical N and Z** come from the logical result, not through the
  adder's result multiplexer.

## The register's clock

**Neither register adds a clock.** Each is loaded from the next state
of the terms it replaces, so it equals them on every clock, as the core's
assertions confirm. The 3/4/5 windows hold, and no path costs a clock
(see Cycles).

**What a register a clock late would break.** The trust belongs to the
access, not to the FSM's state.
- **In a hit-under-miss slot**, `hum_slot_nxt` is computed from the
  access entering the lookup (`line_safe_nxt`: not the victim, not the
  filling line).
- **The departing victim:** a register loaded a clock late would apply
  the previous access's verdict to this one. A hit on the departing
  victim right after a safe access, or as the FSM leaves IDLE, would be
  trusted. That is the hole fixes 1 and 2 closed.
- **The fill end:** the late register would mask the clock after the
  fill-end clock instead of that clock.

**`arm9_trust_sweep_rtl` pins both terms.**
- **The sweep:** a trigger miss, a gap of 0-13 instructions (counted from
  the trigger's issue or from its first word), then a load whose short
  hit must not be accepted. The load is followed at distance 1 or 2 by a
  writer of its register that also reads it:
  - the writers: `add`, `subs` with its flags, a shifted `mov`, a
    dependent `ldr`;
  - the targets: the departing victim (clean or dirty, after a load or a
    store miss), the filling line, and a resident line of another set.
- **The size:** 4,704 cases, each run cold and warm. It runs at the bench
  latency and at 8 and 24, and at 0:0/0:3/2:9.
- **Coverage:** per run, about 2,000 clocks have an unaccepted W hit with
  such a writer waiting in A, in S, or in S as an early operand. Of these
  clocks, 56, 17 and 1 are fill-end clocks at 0:0, 0:3 and 2:9 (a
  temporary probe).

**Mutants of the trust** (failing cases of 4,704 at 0:0 / 0:3 / 2:9):

| Trust | Sweep | `dep_waw` | `install_edge`, `store_reload` |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| register, one clock late | 1,236 / 1,350 / 1,439 | pass | pass |
| none | 1,320 / 1,441 / 1,512 | fails | pass |
| no fill-end term | 6 / 0 / 0 | pass | pass |
| no live term | 1,314 / 1,440 / 1,512 | fails | pass |

- **Where the failures are:** every one is a departing-victim case
  (writers at distance 1, and the early writers at 2).
- **The fill-end term** is caught only by the sweep: a store-miss
  trigger at gap 10, at 0:0.
- **The late register fails almost as many cases as no trust at all.**
  The hit-under-miss slots make the verdict change from one access to
  the next.

## B2

**Not done: the slack does not need it in the form the plan gives.**
- **What B2 would remove:** only the load word from flag-setting
  consumers (`ldr; cmp`).
- **That path is no longer the family's worst.** Family B's worst on the
  r3 device is `backend_m` at −1.951. It starts at the late select itself
  and carries M's and W's ALU results as well. The load word into the Z
  flag is at −1.683, and into `flags` −1.263. B2 would leave the −1.951
  path where it is.
- **Its cost:** step 0 measured 0.6-2.3 % of clocks.
- **What remains of family B:** A's late multiplexer → ALU → logical
  zero tree → Z select → `m`/`flags`/`skid`, at −1.5 to −1.95 on the
  device. Most of it is routing: two 2.2 ns hops in r2's leader.
- **The next device leaders are other families** (the r3 list above):
  - the early load consumer through S's rotator, with C;
  - D2 control from the TLB's `o_wt`;
  - the front end (E);
  - `wb_flush` (D).
