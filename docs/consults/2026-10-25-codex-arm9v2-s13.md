# ARM9V2 session 13 — execution profile

This is a measurement session on `codex/arm9v2`, starting from S12's
`590dd57`. Observer commit: `a2d6708`. The CPU, caches, generated netlist,
SoC and timing shape are unchanged. ZAP remains the default. No Quartus
run, firmware-image modification, `sys/` edit, merge or push was made.
The requested report filename uses October 25; the server's recorded
launch timestamps are October 24 UTC.

The product is an exclusive clock partition, with overlapping raw evidence
and an instruction mix, for the current D16 configuration. It does not
establish 47 M instructions/s in gameplay. The two new Sonic runs are left
running for that measurement.

## Observer and meaning of the numbers

`sim/profile/cpu_profile_probe.sv` is bound only into Verilator builds.
It reads the implementation and existing debug taps; it has no feedback
to the core and is not a synthesis source. The whole-core bench and CPU
suite share `cpu_profile.h`. The whole-core CSV emits a row every guest
second, cumulative/segment rows at the first executive call and thread
entries, and a final partial interval. The suite uses the existing
post-setup PC markers, plus a marker at `test_pass` to isolate cleanup.

Every clock has one exclusive class and every retired instruction has one
primary mix class. Both sums are checked on output and by
[report.py](../../sim/profile/report.py). Retired clocks take priority;
non-final uop retirement, older data waits, serial/reload holds, dependencies,
fetch starvation, recovery and residual structural bubbles follow in that
order. This is **priority attribution, not a sum of independently removable
stall penalties**. A dependency improvement can expose an existing I miss
as fetch starvation. The raw `e*`, stall/cache counters and retirement-during-
fill columns retain that overlap.

[The observer guide](../../sim/profile/README.md) defines every field,
sampling edge, priority, command, core mapping and limitation. The observer
does not propagate a stall-origin tag through every stage. A D/S hold can
overlap an older retirement and leave a later W bubble after the hold has
ended; that bubble is residual `structural_pipeline`. It is not evidence
that every residual clock is a separate structural defect. In particular:

- D-lock means an actual consumer/destination owner is waiting, not just
  that the global lock vector is nonempty. A background fill alone is not
  a D-wait clock if the core retires or can execute without it.
- Recovery persists until its target retires, rather than ending on an
  unrelated older retirement. Higher-priority stalls can mask its exclusive
  count. W RAS repair and predicted-fetch counters have no exact ZAP tap;
  their ZAP zeros mean unavailable, not no prediction/repair.
- Compare aggregate dependency time across cores. ZAP's issue/shifter
  split cannot reproduce V2's early/late-operand subclasses exactly.
- Miss counts are completed load/store/I line fills, not every failed tag
  lookup. A boundary-straddling fill is counted where it completes.
- Instruction mix uses the original expanded instruction. Thumb BL's
  internal two-half markers are recognized; firmware counting retains
  the existing one-instruction heuristic. Return counts are candidates
  (BX lr, MOV pc lr, LDM pc), not proven function returns.
- V2 packed-record probes depend on field layout. The W-width assertion
  does not detect a same-width reorder. ZAP's PC-to-decoded-word map is
  bench state; its self-modifying-code alias limitation is documented.

Before the first executive call, the instruction count **and every primary
mix bin** are identical: 270,625,878 instructions. V2 uses 337,570,706
clocks versus 386,290,367, removing 48,719,661 clocks: **42.1% of ZAP’s
non-retiring clocks**, but only 12.6% of its total. That accounts for 81.1%
of the saving to EFile. Even an impossible zero-overhead, one-retirement-
per-clock implementation could remove only 29.9% of ZAP’s pre-exec clocks.
The intended 35–50% gameplay gain cannot be judged by that boot phase.

The first-exec→EFile phase saves another 11,359,220 clocks (22.9%) despite
retiring 949,554 more instructions. Fetch/branch recovery and structural
gaps are much smaller; live data/dependency attribution is affected by
overlap and different stage schedules. Do not interpret one class delta
as an isolated mechanism’s counterfactual gain.

## What the mechanisms are delivering

The evidence supports useful schedule improvements, but it does not identify
an unimplemented mechanism worth 35–50% on these workloads.

| Intended mechanism | Evidence delivered | Remaining limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Fetch-cycle predictor | Before first exec, fetch-starvation plus branch/return recovery is 6.45 M clocks versus ZAP's 23.45 M; first-exec→EFile is 2.53 M versus 9.16 M. V2 makes millions of predicted fetch advances. | Entry presence is not prediction correctness. A redirects and W returns still cost clocks. There is no matched ZAP prediction-entry counter, and simultaneous stalls mask some recovery. |
| Two-clock late pickup | Raw scalar windows remain 3/4/5. At 2:9 the cached ARM/Thumb bodies spend 443/284 clocks on dependency versus ZAP's 3,752/2,914. | Miss-dependent consumers still wait; the shorter pipe exposes that wait sooner. Some early holds create later residual W bubbles. Live D+dependency attribution to EFile is almost equal (32.58 M / 32.49 M), so all of the structural saving cannot be assigned to late pickup alone. |
| Miss retention | V2 scalar replay is zero. Before first exec it retires 6.14 M instructions during D fills; it also retires through fills in both cached bodies. | ZAP retires 7.37 M during the same pre-exec D-fill count. V2's retained records still need reservation/reload recovery: 5.28 M exclusive clocks to EFile, another 3.45 M to EStart. Retention avoids flushing but does not make ownership restoration free. |
| LDM/STM streaming | The exact stream/tail windows are unchanged; V2's cached-body non-final-uop and block-sequence counts are smaller. Pre-exec uop+block time is about 1.87 M versus ZAP's 5.21 M. | Multiword instructions necessarily consume word slots. EFile→EStart contains 3.26 M non-final-uop clocks (6.1%); these are not all removable drain overhead. PC loads and partial-LDM protection remain conservative. |

The largest V2 non-retiring categories before first exec are data wait
(27.54 M) and structural/uop/bubbles (25.83 M). In the first-exec→EFile
phase, they are data wait (2.41 M), branch/return recovery (2.06 M), and
structural (1.85 M). EFile→EStart has 4.60 M dependency clocks, 3.45 M
reservation/reload clocks and 3.26 M mandatory/non-final uop retirements;
its residual structural bubble count is only 1.81 M. Serial/CP15 is 0.03%
and 0.55% in the two busy phases, and multiply is negligible. There is no
near-zero class unexpectedly consuming tens of percent that establishes
an outright CPU defect. No core fix was made.

There is an additional **polling limitation** in the requested later boot
phase. V2 executes 35,637,236 instructions from EFile to EStart versus
ZAP's 17,699,222, but both execute exactly 886,070 Thumb instructions,
25,293 SWIs and 419 multiplies. The extra 17,938,014 instructions are ARM
work, with a repeated load/store/block/branch mix. This strongly suggests
additional polling iterations; the observer does not provide a PC-frequency
histogram to name that loop conclusively. It is not evidence that V2 did
twice the useful startup work. Milestone progress and game frames remain
the goal, not a high count of timer-bound polling instructions.

**Next-change recommendation, not implemented:** if a bounded CPU experiment
is required before the game results, retain the younger cache lookup owner
and response across a W hold, so successful restoration need not always
spend both RELOAD0 and RELOAD1. Preserve fill-installation relookup and abort
priority. This addresses the largest clearly avoidable control bucket in
the later boot, rather than adding a second miss engine to a phase with
only 2.15% direct D wait. A planning estimate of removing half the exclusive
reservation class is **2.64 M execution clocks to EFile (0.70%)**, including
**0.367 M in first-exec→EFile (0.96%)**, and **1.73 M in EFile→EStart (3.23%)**.
The hard observed ceilings are the full class counts, 5.28 M and 3.45 M;
the estimate assumes half those clocks can legally disappear and is not a
measured counterfactual. Timer-bound polling can turn that execution saving
into more loop iterations rather than an earlier milestone. This is a
small experiment, not a claimed route to the missing 35–50%.

For the cached random bodies, the limiting class is memory service instead:
D waits plus fetch starvation consume 74.2% / 64.6% at 2:9. V2's Thumb
body also completes 196 I fills versus ZAP's 140 despite equal retired mix;
extra speculative fetch traffic is worth tracing before changing the
predictor. The counter alone does not prove a cache or predictor defect.
Eliminating *all* first-exec→EFile I-cache-starvation time would save only
95,374 clocks (0.25% of that phase), not a large percentage of boot.
S12's early-word
counterfactual already established that the boot gain from earlier words
was tiny.

Wait for the 200–235 s Sonic profile before committing to a larger design
change. This session explains the boot percentage and why these random
proxies hide schedule gains; it does not establish the gameplay bottleneck
or an achievable 47 M/s. The profiler's residual bubble bucket and unequal
polling work limit causal conclusions, and are stated rather than filled
with an assumed diagnosis.

## Exact stopping point

The bounded firmware runs are complete: V2 4.5 seconds, ZAP 5.2 seconds
with a common 4.5-second boundary. Both reach EFile and EStart with zero
D/P/U or memory-protocol errors. At their endpoints both report 2,633,612

The instrumentation, 282-run checks in all three configurations, 71 QEMU
references, 89 timing windows at every latency in both V2 modes, lints,
generation check, full boot/suite breakdowns, overhead benchmark and Sonic
launch are complete. No cycle-model or timing change, and no claimed CPU
bug fix, is part of this session. The existing S12 timing result remains
**-2.951 ns / 13,262 ALMs**, not a new S13 fit; timing still does not meet
104 MHz. The measurement does not waive that constraint.

Stop on a clean commit. Leave only the two S13 Sonic jobs running, alongside
the untouched S10 jobs. At the final status check (2026-10-24 14:36:40 UTC), both S13 jobs were
alive with progress through guest second 4; they had not reached play.
The next evidence to read is their actual play
window and LCD progress after guest second 200; no gameplay result exists
in this report. Prioritize that profile over another speculative core
feature. If the residual bubble class dominates play, add stage-propagated
bubble provenance or a targeted PC histogram before treating its clocks as
a particular hazard's removable cost.
